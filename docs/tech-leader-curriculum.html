The Board Verdict

A 12-Month Curriculum

Aug 2026 – Jul 2027  ·  convened 27 July 2026  ·  v2, with links

What should Ruby study over the next 12 months to become a top-tier technology leader?

Six seats argued it independently — an executive search partner, a twice-CTO architect, a learning scientist, a transformation director seated as designated sceptic, an executive coach/spiritual director, and a former EGM now sitting as a non-executive director. Each was briefed with your TELOS and your actual behavioural data, and each was asked to name where the others were wrong.

What they agreed on

1. This is not a knowledge gap. Five of six said it unprompted, in almost the same words.

2. The Masters in Data Science and AI should be killed — 6/6. Not deferred. Killed, in writing, this month.

"Never once in 200 placements has a Masters broken a tie at EGM level." — Kingmaker

"It signals 'still becoming technical' precisely when you need to signal 'already commercial.' The ambiguity is costing you more than the course would give." — Operator

"Two years to be taught gradient descent by someone who has shipped less than you have." — Architect

"Your own system has been telling you it's dead. Believe it." — Practitioner

This contradicts a stated TELOS long-term goal, so it is your call, not theirs. Give it one 20-minute session in week one — enrol or kill. Either outcome ends the ambient guilt that is currently taxing every other slot.

3. The spine is artefacts shipped to named humans, not books read.

"Every other priority in your life is attached to a cue, a person, or a mat with someone trying to choke you. Study is attached to nothing."

"Measurement is not a contingency. Reading 'Masters: 1x in 7d' produces the appropriate discomfort, and the feeling of having a system that's on it substitutes for the act."

4. The build compulsion is the delivery mechanism — with one hard boundary. PAI/Sage at 236 mentions in 24 hours against Masters at 5 in 7 days is arithmetic, not weakness. So the curriculum borrows the loop — but no building study infrastructure. No SRS app, no curriculum tracker, no reading-list agent, no ISA spec for the syllabus. If you build the tool, you have already lost the year.

Where they split

Architect wanted deep systems builds. Steward called technical depth "your comfort, not your gap" — Kingmaker and Operator sided with him. The ruling: the 90-day PAI/Sage feature freeze stands, but reclaimed hours go to work-facing builds only. tms-sim serves Australia Post; a new Sage feature serves your nervous system.

And the one neither business seat let you hide behind:

"Humility that withholds information from decision-makers is not integrity. It is a tax on your own team. You are not selling yourself — you are giving your sponsor ammunition."

The honest time budget

3 × 25 min — Tue/Wed/Thu, between prayer and training
1 × 90 min — Saturday 07:00–08:30
≈165 min/week ≈ 130 hours/year. That is all you have. Any plan asking for more is lying to you.

Not before Bible study — never touch the load-bearing wall. Not evenings, they belong to Vanya. Not Sunday. Study sits after your strongest existing cue and before a reward you will not skip, so training becomes the reinforcer.

The implementation intention, verbatim:
"After I close my Bible and before I put my shoes on, I open ONE file and work 25 minutes."

Nouns don't fire. Every other priority in your stack is a verb with a location — open Bible, tie belt, pick up camera. This one needs to be too.

Before anything goes in — the 90-day subtraction

  • Kill the Masters — in writing, 20 minutes. Enrol or kill; end the ambiguity either way.
  • PAI/Sage feature freeze — 90 days, maintenance only. This is the intervention.
  • Park the ultra comeback — BJJ 3x/week and CrossFit stay as maintenance.
  • Park men's mental health advocacy — 12 months, with an activation condition.
  • Delete the LinkedIn agile course — stale since March. Carrying it is pure drag.
  • Name the Melbourne move as work — 345x across 238 contexts. Make it explicit with Vanya.
  • Vanya's written consent — the whole load gets her explicit yes, and she is told she is expected to use the veto.

Photography and guitar stay. Both are recovery, not load. Cutting them is how you would cheat.

The Curriculum

Four quarters. Each produces one flagship artefact, shipped to a named human, on a date. Reading is ammunition for the artefact, never the point.

Q1 · Aug–Oct 2026 — Commercial Credibility

The highest-ROI quarter, per both business seats.

Flagship artefact: TMS Benefits Realisation Baseline (2 pages). Rebuild the approved board case yourself, from first principles:

  • Separate capex from opex and know why — capitalised build flatters current-year EBIT and creates a depreciation drag from go-live. That is the cliff nobody has modelled on a 30-month programme.
  • Tag every claimed benefit: cashable / cost-avoidance / fiction.
  • Name a benefit owner for each — outside IT.
  • Identify cost-to-serve per parcel and where TMS actually moves it.

Ship to: the CFO's finance business partner — ask them to tear it apart. Then McNamara.

Primary sources (free, and the highest-value reading in the quarter)

  • Australia Post Annual Report 2025 — segment reporting, CSO cost disclosure, capex profile. Line by line.
  • Statement of Corporate Intent 2025/26–2028/29 — what the shareholder ministers have actually been promised.
  • Corporate Governance Statement 2025 — board committee structure; who decides what.
  • Publications index — prior years, for trend analysis.

Books

  • Berman & Knight — Financial Intelligence — the standard text for reading a P&L, balance sheet and cash flow cold.
  • Flyvbjerg & Gardner — How Big Things Get Done — the only book with actual data on why megaprojects fail. Reference-class forecasting applies straight to your phased rollout.

LinkedIn Learning

  • Finance for Non-Financial Managers — Jim Stice. Balance sheet, income statement, cash flows, budgets, costing.
  • Corporate Finance for Non-Financial Managers — capital structure and investment decisions; the capex/opex material.
  • Finance for Managers Professional Certificate — the full path if you want the credential marker.

Arena rep: take one carrier negotiation yourself. Carrier onboarding is commercial and it is your only available commercial rep. You are currently missing it.

Q2 · Nov 2026 – Jan 2027 — The Narrative

Flagship artefact: the ToM six-pager. Prose. No slides. Six pages. Written by you, alone, in the protected morning block. No consultant, no deck team, no working group. Then signed by McNamara, Plant and Bannister.

"This is your FY26 goal, your alignment mechanism, your succession tool and your EGM audition simultaneously. If you cannot do that, no curriculum on earth will help you." — Practitioner

Secondary artefact: Operation Daylight board paper (1 page). Decision sought, options rejected and why, risk, cost, what happens if we do nothing. Papers get deferred when they present analysis instead of a decision, hide the downside, or arrive un-pre-briefed. Rehearse the hostile question in 40 seconds: "460 shadow applications — how would we know if a breach had already happened?"

Ship to: all three EGMs. Separately, take the 460 apps to the CRO before it becomes an incident.

Primary sources

  • Post Office Horizon IT Inquiry — reports — required reading on how technology programmes destroy executives in postal organisations. Volume 1 rapid-read (166pp PDF) is the efficient entry point. Later volumes were still pending as of end-2025 — check the news page for current status.
  • ANAO performance audit reports — how Australian government IT programmes get audited, and what auditors look for.
  • ANAO — ICT procurement panels audit — $2.8bn of arrangements assessed for value-for-money. Directly relevant to Daylight.
  • ANAO Annual Audit Work Program 2025–26 — what is coming. Know before it arrives.

Books

  • Heifetz & Linsky — Leadership on the Line — read the chapter on hungers.

LinkedIn Learning

  • Communicating with Executives — Dan Shapero, LinkedIn COO. What executives want in the first 60 seconds.
  • Developing Executive Presence — John Ullmen, UCLA Anderson. The four factors, with research behind them.
  • Strategic Thinking — Dorie Clark.
  • Executive Leadership topic hub — for change-management modules.

Arena rep: once this quarter, pre-brief all three EGMs and then still present a recommendation you know one of them will hate — and hold it. "Never surprise" must not become "never contest."

Q3 · Feb–Apr 2027 — Systems Judgement Under Failure

Where the build compulsion is legitimately spent.

Flagship artefact: TMS Failure Register + pre-mortem dated 2028. Thirty failure modes across the consignment lifecycle: detection, blast radius, mitigation, named owner. Plus the pre-mortem written as "it failed, here is why."

Optionally backed by tms-sim — a discrete-event simulation in TypeScript: depots, carriers, consignment state machine, injected faults (carrier API timeouts, duplicate scans, clock skew, partial batch failure). Run game days against it.

"Your personal systems are single-user and fail silently at 3am with no consequence. A parcel scanned twice is not an idempotency abstraction — it is a truck going to the wrong depot." — Architect

Ship to: Howard Grace and the TMS steerco. This makes you the only person in the room who can say "here is what breaks, here is when, here is what it costs."

Papers — free, and read these before any book

  • Huang et al., Gray Failure: The Achilles' Heel of Cloud-Scale Systems (HotOS '17) — PDF · MSR page
  • Bronson et al., Metastable Failures in Distributed Systems (HotOS '21) — PDF · ACM DL
  • Dean & Barroso, The Tail at Scale (CACM 2013) — CACM · PDF
  • Fowler, Strangler Fig Application — martinfowler.com
  • Metastable Failures in the Wild — USENIX ;login:

Books

  • Kleppmann — Designing Data-Intensive Applications — chapters 5–9 only. Replication, partitioning, transactions, the trouble with distributed systems, consistency and consensus.
  • Andrew Jones — Driving Data Quality with Data Contracts — Daylight's real problem is 460 undocumented interfaces with no owner and no schema guarantee.

LinkedIn Learning

  • DevOps Foundations: Site Reliability Engineering — Mueller & Wickett. SLAs/SLOs, incident management, retrospectives, distributed system design.

Also this quarter, one page: a unit-cost model — cost per parcel, per scan, per carrier onboarded — you can put in front of three EGMs.

Arena rep: personally run the post-incident review on the worst P1 of the year, in front of the affected EGM, without a comms filter.

Q4 · May–Jul 2027 — Leverage and Succession

Flagship artefact: succession memos. One page per direct report: what they need to be ready in 24 months. Three named successors, each with a stretch assignment and a date. Hand one of them a budget line and a board presentation.

Three seats made the same argument independently: you cannot be promoted out of a role no one else can hold. Your stated legacy goal is also, by some distance, your strongest career move. Search consultants ask "who did you develop?" — and then ring them.

Secondary artefact: the value-creation record (2 pages). Not a CV. Quantified: TMS business case value, Front Door consolidation savings across 460+ apps, P1/P2 SLA delta, cost-to-budget variance. Plus six named referees who will take a cold call.

Books

  • Marquet — Turn the Ship Around! — intent-based leadership, aimed squarely at the legacy goal.
  • Charan, Drotter & Noel — The Leadership Pipeline — the function-leader → business-leader transition you are currently stalled inside.
  • Smart & Street — Who — hiring and succession.

LinkedIn Learning

  • Succession Planning — mission-critical roles, talent review sessions, succession gaps, and preparing the presentation for the board. Maps directly onto the artefact.

The credential decision

AICD Company Directors Course — five days, ~$9,600 + GST, GAICD post-nominal, includes 12 months' AICD membership. In Australia this is the credential that appears on GBE and ASX shortlists. Cohorts run in-person and online through 2026 — book a specific date rather than "sometime." The Kingmaker's view: this is the one credential worth buying, it is short enough to survive your attention pattern, and it puts you in a room with directors, which is the actual product.

External moves

  • One coffee each with three ANZ tech-exec search partners — Egon Zehnder, Derwent, Blenheim Partners. No role in play. One question: "I want an EGM seat in 24 months, tell me what's missing."
  • One real speaking slot: Gartner Symposium ANZ, an ADAPT forum, or a supply-chain/public-sector CIO forum. One. Not LinkedIn posting.

The Continuous Track — Formation

Runs underneath all twelve months. Not a quarter, not optional.

"You built a system that measures everything, and the one thing it cannot see is you." — Steward
  • Daily — fixed morning office, 25 min, same chair, same time. Scripture and prayer, phone in another room. Examen, 5 min, nightly.
  • Weekly — Sabbath: 24 hours, phone in a drawer. Vanya keeps the record, not you. Plus 90 minutes with Vanya, fixed, "state of us": half governance, half not.
  • Fortnightly — teach one concept to your leadership team. Ten minutes, no slides. Highest-yield retrieval practice in existence, costs zero new hours, and directly serves the legacy goal.
  • Monthly — a spiritual director (someone unimpressed by your title), an executive coach (a different person), and an external mentor who has held an EGM seat.
  • First 90 days — Hogan Development Survey (measures derailers directly), a real 360 from all three EGMs and four directs, and Kegan & Lahey — Immunity to Change: map one competing commitment. The Steward called this the hardest technical work of the year.
  • Two silent 3-day retreats. Phone surrendered. First within 60 days.
  • A written Rule of Life — one page, drafted with Vanya, reviewed quarterly.

Formation texts — six across the year, each answered in writing to a peer

  • Nouwen — In the Name of Jesus — the temptations to be relevant, spectacular, powerful. Written for you.
  • Barton — Strengthening the Soul of Your Leadership — leading from a settled rather than a striving place.
  • Peterson — A Long Obedience in the Same Direction — against the instant.
  • Heschel — The Sabbath — rest as architecture, not indulgence.
  • Keller — The Meaning of Marriage — marriage as a leadership discipline.
  • Palmer — Let Your Life Speak — vocation vs. the job.

The marriage is in the curriculum, not adjacent to it. Quarterly, Vanya scores your presence 1–10 and that number is the programme's primary KPI. Your self-report is inadmissible. Any new commitment over two hours a week requires her explicit written yes.

Feedback — named people, scheduled, uncomfortable

  • Three EGMs, quarterly, 30 min — "What did I make harder for you this quarter?" and "What did you hear from someone other than me that you should have heard from me?"
  • Howard Grace, monthly — "Where am I in your way? What are you protecting me from?"
  • Direct reports, 6-monthly, written and anonymous — "Which decision of mine made no sense to you?"
  • CFO / CRO / GC / CPO, quarterly — benefits credibility · risk before incident · Post+ privacy exposure · succession.
  • Frontline, quarterly — one full night sort shift, one retail shift. DNM and DSM are abstractions until you have stood in the facility at 2am.

The one conversation that changes the structure of the year — 45 minutes with McNamara. Enterprise Services owns the budget line and is the one with a real path. Ask, on the record:

"What would have to be true for me to be a credible EGM candidate in 24 months, and would you back me?"

Write the answer down verbatim. Triple accountability means your career is nobody's job — three mentors, zero sponsors. A mentor advises you in the room with you; a sponsor spends their own capital in the room you are not in.

Tripwires and recovery

Designed on the assumption that you will miss. Missing is not the failure — the restart is the habit.

  1. Two consecutive missed sessions → floor protocol within 48 hours: 10 minutes, one page, one paragraph written, in the normal slot. Never three.
  2. 21 days with no artefact shipped to a human → the real failure. Sessions are input; artefacts are the system.
  3. The displacement signature: PAI commits > 20 in a week while study sessions = 0. This is the one number Sage should surface. Nothing else.
  4. Never catch up. Catching up is why people quit. Say this out loud in month one so it is pre-decided rather than negotiated in a bad week.
  5. Recovery red/yellow → the first thing that drops is building. Never sleep, never sabbath, never Vanya.

Sage's role, and its limits. Hold the four artefact ship-dates. Surface the displacement signature. That is all. Explicitly not more nudges — every suggested action you declined taught the system that its nudges carry no cost. That channel is burnt.

The test at twelve months

Not "did he read the books." Five answers, three of which you do not get to see first.

  1. Vanya on "are you more present than a year ago?" — the primary KPI.
  2. Your directs on "is he easier to disagree with?"
  3. The sabbath ledger Vanya keeps.
  4. Four artefacts shipped and signed — benefits baseline, ToM six-pager, failure register, succession memos.
  5. One named person has said "yes, I'll back you."
"If those come back right, you'll be ready for EGM. If they don't, the title will arrive and you won't be there to receive it." — Steward

Continuous Learning — Year Two and Beyond

The twelve months above is a programme. What follows is an operating system. The point of the first year is to install a cadence you never have to re-decide.

The permanent cadence

  • Quarterly — one artefact, shipped to a named human, on a date. Forever. Rotate the lens (commercial, narrative, systems, people) but never drop to zero. This is the whole system.
  • Fortnightly — teach one thing to your leadership team. Ten minutes, no slides. Never stops.
  • Monthly — spiritual director · executive coach · external mentor. Three separate people, three different jobs.
  • Annually — re-baseline TELOS against actual behavioural data, not intentions. Refresh the value-creation record whether or not you are looking.

Standing inputs — the self-renewing reading diet

These renew themselves. You never need a new reading list again.

  • Every August — the organisation's own numbers. Australia Post's Annual Report and the updated Statement of Corporate Intent, read the week they drop, before anyone briefs you on them. Two hours. Compounds more than any book.
  • Every year — the audit and accountability layer. The ANAO Annual Audit Work Program tells you what will be scrutinised next; ANAO performance audits tell you how comparable programmes actually failed. Free, specific, and almost nobody at your level reads it.
  • Continuously — engineering judgement. AWS Builders' Library (timeouts, retries, jitter, backpressure) · ACM Queue, one article a month · martinfowler.com, whose legacy-modernisation tag stays relevant for the whole Daylight arc · the DORA State of DevOps report annually — the only credible measurement instrument for "velocity high," which is currently a vibe.
  • Continuously — the outside view. One serious industry publication read weekly rather than five skimmed. Rotate every couple of years so you don't calcify.

Skill decay — what to refresh, and when

  • Every year — financial fluency, on live material. Rebuild one business case from first principles annually, a different one each time. It decays fast and it is the thing that gets tested.
  • Every 2 years — a 360 with fresh raters, and a re-run of the Hogan derailer profile. Derailers move under load.
  • Every 2–3 years — technical currency in one area you have started hand-waving about. Pick it by noticing where you have stopped asking questions.
  • Every 3–5 years — the peer network. Relationships that go unrefreshed for five years are archaeology, not a network.

The permanent board

The six seats that debated this were a device, but the structure is worth keeping as real people:

  • A commercial voice — CFO or a finance director who will tell you your numbers are wrong.
  • A technical peer — someone still building, who can tell you when you are hand-waving.
  • A political read — an external mentor who has held an EGM seat.
  • A formation voice — the spiritual director.
  • A truth-teller from below — a direct report who is not frightened of you.
  • Vanya — with the veto.

Refresh the composition every two to three years. Anyone who has stopped disagreeing with you has stopped being useful.

What the board cut — permanently

  • The Masters in Data Science and AI — 6/6, kill it
  • The LinkedIn Learning agile/sprint course — stale since March; delete
  • Vendor certifications (AWS SA Pro et al.) — pure signalling for someone with a shipping record
  • A twelve-book reading list — ~50 passive hours against a 130-hour budget, dead by week six. Four books read twice with written retrieval beats twelve read once. Every time.
  • Generic executive-education / MBA modules — content is downstream of what you already do. If you go, go for the cohort; be honest that the cohort is the entire product.
  • Any evening study block — fantasy. Cut on sight.
  • New Sage/PAI features for 90 days — the freeze is the intervention.
